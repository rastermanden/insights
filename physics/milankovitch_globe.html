How Milankovitch Cycles Drive Ice Ages

Serbian mathematician Milutin Milanković showed that slow, periodic changes in Earth's orbit alter how much solar energy reaches each latitude — and when. Three cycles interact:

  • Obliquity (41 kyr cycle, 22.1°–24.5°) — the tilt of Earth's axis. Lower tilt means weaker summers at high latitudes: less seasonal melt, so ice sheets advance toward the equator.
  • Eccentricity (100 kyr / 400 kyr) — how elliptical the orbit is. Higher eccentricity amplifies the insolation difference between perihelion and aphelion, making seasons in one hemisphere more extreme than the other.
  • Precession (26 kyr) — the wobble of Earth's spin axis, which determines which solstice falls near perihelion. When the Northern Hemisphere's summer coincides with perihelion, it gets extra-warm summers and the northern ice cap retreats; the southern cap correspondingly grows.

Ice-Albedo Feedback

Ice reflects ~75 % of incoming sunlight; open ocean and land reflect only ~10–30 %. As ice expands, more solar energy is returned to space, cooling the planet further and causing more ice growth — a runaway positive feedback that amplifies the weak orbital forcing into full glacial cycles.

This model computes an effective planetary albedo:

\[ \alpha_\text{eff} = 0.30 + f_\text{ice} \times 0.45 \]

where \( f_\text{ice} \) is the fraction of Earth's surface covered by polar ice, and 0.45 is the extra reflectivity contribution of ice above the ice-free baseline of 0.30.

Stefan-Boltzmann Equilibrium Temperature

The equilibrium temperature is found by balancing absorbed solar flux with outgoing long-wave radiation:

\[ T = \left( \frac{\bar{S}\,(1 - \alpha_\text{eff})}{4\,\sigma} \right)^{\!1/4} - 273.15 \]

Mean annual insolation accounting for orbital eccentricity \( e \) (the orbit integral gives an exact result):

\[ \bar{S} = \frac{S_0}{\sqrt{1 - e^2}} \]

where \( S_0 = 1361\,\text{W m}^{-2} \) is the solar constant and \( \sigma = 5.670 \times 10^{-8}\,\text{W m}^{-2}\text{K}^{-4} \) is the Stefan-Boltzmann constant. The result is a no-atmosphere effective temperature; the real Earth is ~33 °C warmer due to the greenhouse effect.
